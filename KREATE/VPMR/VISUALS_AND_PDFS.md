## Visuals / diagrams

### EPA Wasted Food Scale

- Canonical page: https://www.epa.gov/sustainable-management-food/wasted-food-scale
- EPA provides downloadable visual versions on the page.
- Good use: pitch/research explanation of prevention hierarchy.
- Boundary: it is a US EPA hierarchy graphic, not a Boğaziçi result or Turkish legal mandate.
- Credit/reuse: use publisher-provided files and retain EPA/source credit.

### UNEP Food Waste Index 2024 visuals

- Publication page: https://www.unep.org/resources/publication/food-waste-index-report-2024
- Repository/download: https://wedocs.unep.org/handle/20.500.11822/45230
- Useful visuals include report cover/scope/measurement diagrams.
- Prefer linking or quoting a small credited figure only where license/usage permits; do not copy arbitrary web thumbnails.

### Boğaziçi public operational visuals

Official university/dining pages may contain photos, menus or page graphics. Treat them as source-page visuals. Do not duplicate photographs into the repository unless reuse terms are clear; store the canonical page link and a caption describing why it matters.

## Academic figures

Use figures from open-access papers only after checking the article license. Prefer referencing the figure/article over copying it. If a figure is copied for internal research:

1. store exact article/figure citation;
2. record license;
3. keep original caption/context;
4. never present an external figure as BOUNCAMPUS data.

## Screenshot evidence

For time-sensitive web pages, a future agent may preserve a dated screenshot **only** as a source snapshot, with URL + retrieval time + hash/provenance. A screenshot is evidence of what the page showed, not evidence that the operational claim behind it is true.

## Why binaries are not vendored in this initial pass

The source pages/PDFs are stable and directly linkable, while binary copying creates license, staleness and repository-size risk. The registry therefore keeps canonical links and explicit reuse notes; a later evidence snapshot can vendor a file only when reproducibility requires it.
