## Five Core Execution Questions

1. **IE — Customer Discovery & Market Lead:** What is actually happening in the market and operation?
2. **EE — Physical Systems & Measurement Lead:** What should we measure, and can we trust the physical measurement?
3. **EHB — Embedded Hardware, Communications & Integration Lead:** Can we turn those requirements into reliable electronics/firmware and connect them correctly to software?
4. **CS1 — Decision Intelligence Lead:** Can we help the operator make a better decision and prove it?
5. **CS2 — Product Strategy, Evidence Synthesis & Application Lead:** Given the evidence, what should we build and how do we defend the thesis?

## Role Count Is Not Human Count

The project still has four human team members and the PMR operating target remains **16 distinct high-quality interviews**, approximately four lead interviews per person. EHB is an execution/ownership role and may be staffed by the appropriate human plus autonomous agents; adding EHB does not fabricate a fifth person or expand the PMR tracker to 20 slots.

## EE ↔ EHB Boundary

EE owns measurement correctness: measurand, sensor/measurement strategy, calibration, uncertainty, placement, measurement semantics, and field validity.

EHB owns embedded implementation: electronics, controller/PCB, firmware, communications, buffering/recovery, bring-up, device protocol, and HW↔SW integration.

Changes across their boundary use an explicit EE↔EHB interface contract and dual review.

## Shared Rules

1. Any role may cross into another workstream when strategically useful.
2. Any role may open a bounded experiment without waiting for routine approval.
3. PMR is shared across the four human team members; execution roles do not imply one human per role.
4. Evidence may change the product, beachhead, feature set, hardware plan or model strategy.
5. Role documents are guides for judgment and ownership, not fixed checklists.
6. Important claims must remain distinguishable as fact, measurement, estimate, interpretation or hypothesis.
7. AI is encouraged for speed and rigor, but never as a substitute for real evidence.
8. EE/EHB interface changes must not silently alter voltage, pinout, sampling, protocol, schema, power budget, calibration persistence, or fault semantics.
