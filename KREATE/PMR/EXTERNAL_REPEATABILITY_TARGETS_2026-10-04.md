# Selection principle

Do not choose external PMR targets merely because a contact is easy to find.

Prefer institutions that deliberately test different hypotheses:

```text
same operational problem?
same user?
same decision timing?
same payment/risk structure?
same data maturity?
same adoption path?
```

A useful external sample should include both **similarity** and **variation**.

# P0 External Target — Gebze Technical University

## Why GTÜ is unusually high-information

GTÜ's official Beslenme Hizmetleri page, last updated **10 September 2026**, publicly states that:

- food-production quantities are determined using **historical consumption data**;
- **daily user counts** are considered;
- planning explicitly aims to prevent food waste;
- unexpected demand increases can cause some high-demand meals to **run out early**;
- in those cases, food is rapidly replenished to maintain service;
- food service is monitored by an SKS food-inspection team including **1 food engineer, 1 dietitian and 2 cooks**.

Official source:
https://www.gtu.edu.tr/kategori/5904/0/display.aspx

The page also publicly lists responsible branch-manager roles for dining areas.

## Why this matters

This is current public evidence for nearly the exact decision tension BOUNCAMPUS is hypothesizing:

```text
historical demand
+ daily user context
        ↓
production quantity
        ↓
unexpected demand
        ↓
early running out
        ↓
replenishment
```

GTÜ is therefore a strong **repeatability test**, not because it proves BOUNCAMPUS is needed, but because it can answer:

- Is the current planning process painful enough to improve?
- How good is the historical/daily-user heuristic already?
- How often is replenishment needed?
- Is the controllable decision the initial quantity or later batch/replenishment?
- What data are retained?
- What is the cost of overproduction vs early depletion?

## Interview role

Ask the responsible SKS dining/beslenme manager to route to the person who actually determines production quantities.

Do not assume the publicly named branch manager is the production planner.

## Highest-value questions

1. "The public page says production uses historical consumption and daily user counts. Who actually converts those signals into the day's quantity?"
2. "At what time does that quantity become difficult to change?"
3. "How often do the high-demand dishes require replenishment?"
4. "When that happens, is the problem total meal count or item mix?"
5. "Which historical window/heuristic do you use today?"
6. "What would a better decision have to improve beyond the current process?"

### Falsification value

If GTÜ already solves this job sufficiently with its current heuristic/workflow, that is important competitive evidence against BOUNCAMPUS's assumed gap.

Its current public directory lists a dedicated **Cafeterias Management** unit with:

- Director — Dilek Keskin Şahbaz;

Current sources:

- https://w3.bilkent.edu.tr/bilkent/cafeterias-management/personnel/

Bilkent's public institutional self-evaluation report describes menu planning involving Cafeterias Management dietitians and food-service company representatives, while considering feedback and observed preference/consumption.

Source:
https://w3.bilkent.edu.tr/web/degerlendirme_raporu/BilkentUniversitesi_Kurum_Ic_Degerlendirme_Raporu_Mart2022.pdf

Current general professional contact:

- `kafemud@bilkent.edu.tr`

## What Bilkent tests

- Does the same production-planning job exist under a foundation-university administrative model?
- Is procurement/pilot access actually easier, or is that an incorrect assumption?
- Does having dedicated dietitians/management change the decision workflow?
- How does vendor coordination work?
- Is the current process already data-rich enough that BOUNCAMPUS adds little?

## Why not treat it as the same segment automatically

Bilkent has many dining outlets and different service concepts. The table-d'hôte / institutional meal-plan operation must be separated from independent café/retail demand if interviewing.

# P1 External Target — İstanbul Technical University

## Why İTÜ

İTÜ gives a useful **large public + electronically measured consumption + replenishment** comparison.

Official/public sources show:

- every campus has student/personnel dining services;
- menus are prepared under cafeteria-manager control by food engineer/food technician/head-cook roles;
- public procurement records describe electronic card/turnstile consumption records used in contractor payment;
- procurement clauses also describe additional equivalent food preparation when food is at risk of running out.

Sources:

- https://global.itu.edu.tr/tr/itu-de-ya%C5%9Fam/yeme-icme-ve-alisveris
- public procurement decision mirror: https://herpoz.com/kamu-ihale-kararlari/2026UH.II-740-kamu-ihale-karari-kik

## What İTÜ tests

- Is actual consumption data accessible to operations before/after service?
- Is the key decision initial quantity or adaptive replenishment?
- How does electronic settlement affect contractor incentives?
- Does a sophisticated large institution still have an unresolved forecasting/decision gap?

## Interview route

Prefer current dining/cafeteria management via the institution rather than the academic Food Engineering Department, unless the operational team identifies a specific technical staff member.

# What counts as repeatability evidence

Do not count:

- a public webpage;
- a contact reply saying the idea sounds interesting;
- another university having a cafeteria;
- another university measuring waste.

Count a workflow as meaningfully repeated only when an interview can reconstruct:

```text
role
-> pre-service inputs
-> quantity/batch decision
-> freeze point
-> error consequence
-> actual-demand record
-> current workaround
-> unresolved pain
```

and it is sufficiently similar that the same core product and sales story could plausibly serve both institutions.

# Contact discipline

Public professional contact information is for bounded research outreach only.

- start with one appropriate role per institution;
- request routing to the actual decision owner;
- do not mass-message multiple staff simultaneously;
- do not claim institutional partnership/approval;
- do not request personal student data;
- do not pitch a long feature list before understanding the workflow.
