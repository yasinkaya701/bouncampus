# Boğaziçi Food Procurement & Contract Research — 2026–2027

**Research date:** 2026-10-01  
**Purpose:** Map the current public procurement structure behind Boğaziçi dining so KREATE agents understand the economic unit, contractor relationship and the exact contract questions that PMR/data access must resolve.  
**Status:** Secondary/public-source research only. **NOT legal advice. NOT PMR. NOT proof of actual invoice/meal acceptance behavior.**

---

# 0. Executive conclusion

The current food-service operation is not an informal cafeteria process. Boğaziçi's 2026–2027 food service is defined through a large public **unit-price service procurement** covering millions of meal units across six campuses.

This materially strengthens the current KREATE thesis:

> the production/allocation quantity is an economically meaningful operational unit inside a formal service contract.

But one crucial question remains unanswered by the public notice:

> **Which quantity becomes payable/accepted — forecast, ordered, produced, delivered, turnstile-served, or another contract-defined quantity?**

Until that is resolved, the team must not convert forecast reductions into claimed TL savings.

# 2. Contract quantity is an explicit economic unit

## PROC-02 — The tender is a unit-price contract

The official notice states that bidders submit prices based on:

> each work item's quantity × the unit price offered for that item

and that the successful bidder signs a **unit-price contract**.

This is important because the procurement itself distinguishes meal categories as quantified line items rather than treating the entire service as a single undifferentiated fixed-price operation.

### What this supports

- quantity is contractually relevant;
- student meal, breakfast and staff meal are separate procurement quantities;
- the operational product should distinguish meal/service classes rather than forecast one university-wide number.

### What this does not support

The notice alone does not establish:

- whether the university pays only for actual accepted servings;
- whether daily quantities are called off/order-adjusted;
- whether produced but unserved food is payable;
- whether shortages create deductions or penalties;
- whether monthly progress payments use turnstile, kitchen, delivery or another record;
- the winning unit price for each line item.

Those questions require the contract/technical specification, progress-payment records or owner interview.

Source:  
https://www.ihaledetay.com/2025-1727143

# 3. The procurement explicitly describes a 10,000-meal daily need scale

## PROC-03 — Capacity criterion reveals the procurement's daily design scale

The successful tender notice requires bidders to provide a valid production-capacity report covering **5,000 meal units**, explicitly described as **one-half of the administration's daily meal requirement**.

Therefore, within the procurement wording:

```text
half of daily food requirement = 5,000 meals
implied full daily food requirement = 10,000 meals
```

This should be read as the procurement's capacity/design requirement, **not automatically as observed average daily consumption**.

The same tender notice separately lists 3.13 million meal units over the two-year contract. Public activity reports also use other operational scale figures such as 6,000 daily meals. Those metrics have different definitions and should remain distinct until a source owner reconciles them.

Source:  
https://www.ihaledetay.com/2025-1727143

### Historical corroboration, not current-contract evidence

A prior Public Procurement Board decision concerning an earlier Boğaziçi meal procurement also described a capacity criterion where 5,000 meals represented half the administration's daily food requirement, and 500 breakfasts represented half the daily breakfast requirement. This supports that a high-capacity central production requirement is not a new concept, but it should not be substituted for the current contract wording.

Source:  
https://arsiv.kikkararlari.com/index.php?Itemid=9&id=74669&option=com_content&task=view

# 4. Procurement history contains an important cancelled predecessor

## PROC-04 — Do not mix IKN `2025/1335958` with the successful contract

An earlier tender carrying the same 2026–2027 service title used İKN **2025/1335958**. It was cancelled on 07.10.2025 after objections to the tender documentation led the authority to conclude that specification changes were necessary, while an EKAP amendment could not be issued at that stage.

Source:  
https://ekapveri.com/ihale/ekap-2025-1335958/

The later successful procurement is **2025/1727143**.

### Agent rule

When searching tender documents, screenshots or third-party mirrors, always attach the `İKN` to the extracted fact. Do not merge clauses from the cancelled and successful procedures without verifying that they survived into the final successful tender.

# 5. Price adjustment and contract economics

## PROC-05 — The current procurement indicates price adjustment

EKAP-derived tender records mark the successful procurement as one where **price difference / price adjustment is provided** (`Fiyat Farkı Verilecek`).

Source:  
https://ekapveri.com/ihale/ekap-2025-1727143/

### Product implication

Do not model the economics using one fixed nominal TRY-per-meal value over the entire 2026–2027 period. Ingredient, labor and other indexed contract economics may move.

For a pilot, prioritize **physical operational KPIs** first:

```text
excess portions
shortage portions
served portions
unserved edible surplus
waste kg at a defined boundary
service-level failures
```

Translate to money only with verified contract/payment semantics.

# 7. Highest-value contract questions for PMR

## For Food Services / procurement owner

1. For a normal lunch tomorrow, what quantity is formally communicated to the contractor?
2. At what time is that quantity fixed?
3. Is it an order/call-off under the unit-price contract?
4. Can it be revised after the first communication? Until when?
5. Which quantity is used for monthly acceptance/payment: ordered, produced, delivered, served, or another field?
6. How are student meal, breakfast and staff meal unit prices defined in the signed contract?
7. What deductions/penalties apply for shortages, quality failures or service interruptions?
8. Is excess production paid if it is not served?
9. Is a minimum guaranteed quantity defined by day/month/contract period?
10. Which reports or progress-payment documents reconcile quantities each month?

## For contractor / TEMAŞ operations

1. Who receives the university's daily expected quantities?
2. Who converts them into production batches?
3. When are ingredients committed and when is cooking irreversible?
4. Is campus allocation decided before production or after production?
5. Can the second/third batch react to early turnstile demand?
6. What happens financially and operationally to unserved output?
7. Which field do you wish the university predicted more accurately?

# 8. Data request derived from the unit-price contract

A useful data request should connect operational quantity to contract acceptance without requesting sensitive procurement material unnecessarily.

For an 8–12 week pilot/history window, ask for privacy-safe aggregated fields where available:

```text
DATE
MEAL_TYPE                  # student meal / breakfast / staff meal
CAMPUS
SERVICE_CHANNEL
FORECAST_OR_REQUESTED_QTY  # if a daily call-off exists
REVISED_REQUEST_QTY        # if changes are allowed
PRODUCED_QTY
DELIVERED_QTY
SERVED_OR_ACCEPTED_QTY
CONTRACT_ACCEPTED_QTY      # quantity used in hakediş/payment if releasable
UNSERVED_SURPLUS
WASTE_QTY_WITH_BOUNDARY
SHORTAGE_OR_SERVICE_FAILURE
```

### Why `CONTRACT_ACCEPTED_QTY` matters

If this field differs from produced or served quantity, the economic objective may differ from the physical waste objective. The product must optimize the decision the organization can actually act on, not merely forecast attendance.

# 10. Safe KREATE application language

Before contract PMR, a defensible public-source formulation is:

> `PUBLIC SOURCE` Boğaziçi University's 2026–2027 food-service procurement covers 3.13 million listed meal units across six campuses and is structured as a unit-price service contract. The successful tender also sets production-capacity requirements at a 10,000-meal daily design scale. `HYPOTHESIS` The team is testing whether the daily production/allocation quantity can be adjusted using existing aggregate demand signals before the operational freeze point, and whether that would reduce excess without increasing shortages.

Do **not** write:

- `The university loses 759 million TL to food waste.`
- `Every avoided meal saves 242 TL.`
- `The university pays for every produced-but-unserved meal.`
- `TEMAŞ chooses the quantities.`
- `The contract guarantees a 10,000-meal actual daily volume.`

None of those claims is established by the public notice.

# 12. Bottom line

The procurement research makes the product thesis materially sharper:

```text
not: predict cafeteria traffic for sustainability

but:
identify the contract-relevant production/allocation decision
→ determine its freeze deadline
→ surface privacy-safe demand signals available before that deadline
→ recommend quantity under explicit excess/shortage costs
→ retain human approval
→ measure physical and contract outcomes prospectively
```

The next decisive evidence is not another ML paper. It is the **signed-contract / hakediş quantity semantics and the real daily quantity workflow**.
