# University Dining Persona Role Pattern Research — 2026-10-04

**Status:** Public organizational-role research. **Role recurrence is not proof of daily production-decision ownership.**

---

# Executive conclusion

Across Turkish universities, the organizational labels vary, but a recurring operational structure appears:

```text
institutional food/cafeteria management
        +
food engineer / dietitian / technical food role
        +
service or contractor operations
```

This supports a narrower PMR persona family than `sustainability manager`:

> **Food Services Operations Manager / Production Quantity Decision Owner**

However, the exact user can sit on either the university or contractor side. Public staff titles cannot identify the quantity owner; PMR must follow the decision itself.

# 2. İstanbul Technical University

Official İTÜ campus-services information states that monthly menus are prepared under the **cafeteria manager's control** by a team including:

- food engineer;
- food technician;
- head cook.

Source:
https://global.itu.edu.tr/tr/itu-de-ya%C5%9Fam/yeme-icme-ve-alisveris

Separate public procurement evidence shows contractor payment can be tied to cafeteria/turnstile actual-consumption records and that the contractor may need to produce additional equivalent food if a meal is at risk of running out.

### Persona implication

The actual decision unit can span:

```text
cafeteria manager
+ technical food staff
+ contractor production operator
```

rather than one sustainability or IT role.

# 6. Persona family — role, not title

## Primary operational persona hypothesis

> The person accountable for translating uncertain expected demand into a meal-production/allocation plan while protecting service continuity.

Possible titles:

- Food Services Branch Manager;
- Cafeteria Manager/Director;
- Cafeterias Management Director;
- food engineer/dietitian with production-planning responsibility;
- contractor project manager;
- central-kitchen production manager.

### Required attributes

The true primary persona should satisfy most of:

```text
sees or influences the quantity plan
understands service-risk consequences
has access to current heuristic/data
can approve/change/route the decision
feels operational pain when forecast is wrong
participates in post-service reconciliation
```

Title alone is insufficient.

---

# 7. Decision-making unit hypothesis

For B2B institutional dining, keep these actors separate:

| Role | Candidate actor |
| --- | --- |
| end user | production planner / food engineer / kitchen manager |
| operational owner | Food Services / cafeteria manager / contractor project lead |
| technical influencer | food engineer / dietitian / IT/data owner |
| data owner | BUCard/POS/ERP / contractor operations / waste owner |
| sustainability champion | sustainability / zero-waste unit |
| economic buyer | university administration or contractor depending settlement structure |
| approver | SKS/facilities/procurement/senior operations depending institution |
| beneficiary | budget, contractor margin, students, sustainability program |

### Important

One person can occupy multiple roles, but do not assume this.

---

# 8. Purchasing/use criteria to test, not assert

Based on the responsibility pattern and known operational risks, a candidate priority stack is:

1. **service continuity** — does not create unacceptable shortages;
2. **timing** — recommendation arrives before the real freeze point;
3. **operational simplicity** — does not add a heavy data-entry burden;
4. **reliability / missing-data behavior**;
5. **explainability and human control**;
6. **fit with contractor/current systems**;
7. **measurable physical/economic result**;
8. **privacy/security/procurement burden**;
9. **sustainability/reporting value**.

This ordering is a **PMR hypothesis**. Do not present it as a confirmed persona hierarchy until the persona ranks actual tradeoffs.

---

# 9. Interview strategy derived from role research

The first question should not be:

> "Are you responsible for food waste?"

It should be:

> **"For tomorrow's lunch, who decides how many portions will be prepared, and who changes that number when new information arrives?"**

Then follow the answer to the actual role even if it is:

- a contractor employee;
- a food engineer;
- a kitchen manager;
- a senior administrator;
- a joint committee/workflow.

This prevents persona research from becoming an organization-chart exercise.

---

# 10. Beachhead implication

The repeated role pattern makes a cross-university beachhead plausible **only if** PMR shows the same job and buying process repeat.

A more robust persona definition is therefore:

> **The operational owner of high-volume institutional meal production/allocation under uncertain demand.**

not:

> **a university sustainability manager.**

Sustainability teams remain possible champions/measurement reviewers but should not be treated as the primary end user unless real workflow evidence says otherwise.
