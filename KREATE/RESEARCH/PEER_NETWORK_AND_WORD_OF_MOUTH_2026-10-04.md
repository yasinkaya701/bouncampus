# Peer Network & Word-of-Mouth Research — 2026-10-04

**Purpose:** test the Disciplined Entrepreneurship requirement that beachhead end users have meaningful peer/community communication that can support references and word of mouth.  
**Status:** secondary/public-source evidence only. **A dense national food-services peer network is NOT yet proven.**

## Executive conclusion

There is now direct, current official evidence that Turkish university SKS leaders and branch-level staff compare operating methods and exchange good practices across institutions.

A dated example from **24–25 September 2026** documents an inter-university benchmarking visit between Ankara Yıldırım Beyazıt University and Kırşehir Ahi Evran University. The SKS-specific meeting included department heads, branch managers and staff and explicitly compared duties, working methods and management mechanisms while exchanging good-practice examples.

This materially strengthens the plausibility of a peer-reference channel, but it still does **not** prove that dining-production decision makers form a dense national community or that one BOUNCAMPUS pilot would diffuse automatically.

The word-of-mouth condition remains a PMR hypothesis.

# 1. Direct current evidence of cross-university SKS benchmarking

Ankara Yıldırım Beyazıt University's official SKS page published on **25 September 2026** describes a benchmarking visit held on **24 September 2026** with Kırşehir Ahi Evran University.

Official source:

The source states that the SKS meeting included:

- the two universities' SKS department heads;
- relevant branch managers;
- staff / responsible personnel.

Topics included:

- examples of good practice;
- information and experience sharing;
- unit duties and responsibilities;
- working methods;
- management mechanisms;
- comparative evaluation of institutional processes.

The university-level report describes the broader visit as an **Universitiesarası Kıyaslama / Benchmarking** program intended to identify mutual learning areas and jointly assess improvable processes.

Official source:
https://www.aybu.edu.tr/aybu/tr/haber/23686/AYB%C3%9C%E2%80%99de-%C3%9Cniversiteleraras%C4%B1-K%C4%B1yaslama-Ziyareti%3A-%C4%B0yi-Uygulamalar-ve-Kurumsal-Deneyimler-Payla%C5%9F%C4%B1ld%C4%B1

### What this supports

At least some university administrative/SKS teams:

```text
meet peers
compare operating methods
share management practices
exchange improvement experience
```

This is stronger evidence than merely observing similar department names.

### What it does not support

- a formal national dining-operations network;
- frequent exchange specifically about meal-production forecasting;
- that one successful pilot would automatically spread to peers;
- that all target end users participate in the same network;
- that peer references directly determine procurement decisions.

# 3. Why this matters for BOUNCAMPUS beachhead selection

Disciplined Entrepreneurship treats community connection / word of mouth as one of the defining beachhead conditions.

If Food Services/SKS operators:

- benchmark peer institutions;
- reuse procurement/process ideas;
- ask colleagues for vendor/tool references;
- discuss service-quality practices;
- visit peer campuses to compare workflows;

then one credible pilot can have reference value beyond the original institution.

If this behavior is weak specifically among the production-decision persona, `Turkish universities` may be a customer category but not a strong DE beachhead community.

---

# 4. PMR questions to test network density

Ask every operational Persona candidate:

- Which other universities do you compare your dining operation with?
- When you need a new food-service practice or vendor, who do you ask?
- Which universities' technical specifications or practices do you follow?
- Are there SKS/food-service manager groups, meetings or informal channels you participate in?
- Who has influenced a dining-process change here in the last two years?
- Have you adopted an operational practice because another university demonstrated it?
- If a peer university reduced waste without more shortages, whose result would you trust?
- Which institution would you recommend we interview next?

Record named referrals rather than only `yes, we talk to peers`.

---

# 5. Strong validation signal

The DE word-of-mouth criterion becomes more credible if multiple interviewees independently name overlapping:

```text
peer institutions
professional groups
WhatsApp/email communities
formal benchmarking visits
quality/ISO networks
procurement precedents
shared vendors/contractors
```

and can describe a real practice adopted through that network.

A single official benchmarking visit establishes **community plausibility**, not network density.

---

# 6. Contractor networks may be more important than university networks

Current procurement research shows the same catering firms may operate across multiple institutional sites.

This creates another possible network:

```text
catering operator
-> multiple university accounts
-> repeated production-planning problem
```

If the contractor owns the quantity decision and economics, the stronger beachhead community might be **institutional catering operators**, not universities.

PMR should therefore ask contractor-side stakeholders:

- How many institutional sites use the same planning process?
- Are forecasting/production tools selected centrally or per project?
- Can a successful site pilot transfer to other contracts?
- Who owns technology purchasing at company level?

### Strategic implication

A contractor-level GTM could create faster multi-site leverage if the same product and purchasing process repeat across accounts.

This is currently a hypothesis.

---

# 7. Procurement documents as indirect diffusion

Public-university technical specifications and KİK decisions expose detailed operational clauses and precedents.

This can lead to institutional practices diffusing through:

- specification drafting;
- procurement consultants/vendors;
- legal precedents;
- shared contractor experience.

However, this is not identical to end-user word of mouth and should not be used to claim a community without PMR evidence.

# 9. Beachhead consequence

Do not mark `same community / word of mouth` as fully passed yet.

Instead use cross-site PMR referrals as evidence:

```text
Interview A names B/C as peers
Interview B independently names A/C
A reports adopting a real practice from B
```

The new AYBÜ–KAEU evidence raises the beachhead-community hypothesis from speculative to **plausible and directly testable**, but not validated.
