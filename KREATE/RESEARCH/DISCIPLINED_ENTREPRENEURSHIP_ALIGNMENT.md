## 2. DE beachhead test applied to BOUNCAMPUS

DE says a beachhead must be sufficiently homogeneous that end users:
1. use the same product;
2. can be reached through essentially the same sales process / value proposition;
3. participate in meaningful word-of-mouth / reference networks.

Therefore `large Turkish universities` is still too broad.

### Candidate beachhead v2

> Turkish medium-to-large universities with centrally coordinated or institution-governed dining, recurring high-volume meal services, an identifiable pre-service production-quantity decision, and enough service-level measurement capability to run a bounded operational pilot.

### Further workflow segmentation

The procurement research shows materially different operating models:

A. **Institution-led quantity planning**
- university/food-services estimates quantity;
- contractor executes;
- operational buyer/user may sit inside the university.

B. **Contractor-led quantity risk**
- contractor estimates demand from historical/context data;
- payment may be tied to actual consumption/turnstile counts;
- excess-production risk may sit with contractor.

C. **Reservation + walk-in planning**
- reservations provide a strong intent signal;
- walk-ins/show-no-shows still create uncertainty.

These may not be one homogeneous beachhead if they require different users, product configuration, procurement language, or sales process.

## 3. Proposed beachhead certification questions

Before calling the segment validated, PMR must answer:

### Same product
- Is the core decision object the same: portions/batches to prepare before service?
- Are the necessary inputs broadly similar: historical demand, menu, calendar, reservation/entry signals, operational regime?
- Can the same human-reviewed production recommendation workflow work without institution-specific product rewrites?

### Same sales process
- Is the economic buyer consistently Food Services/SKS, a catering contractor, or another actor?
- Does procurement require direct university sale, contractor partnership, pilot/research agreement, or tender inclusion?
- Does the value proposition remain `reduce avoidable surplus while protecting service continuity`, or does it change materially by procurement model?

### Word of mouth / reference network
- Do university food-service managers, SKS units, food engineers, contractors, or procurement professionals exchange references/best practices across institutions?
- Are sector events, contractor portfolios, YÖK/GreenMetric networks or professional food-service networks realistic reference channels?

Until these are tested, the beachhead remains `TESTING`.

## 5. Persona alignment

DE Step 5 requires a **real person**, not merely a composite job title, and emphasizes prioritized purchasing criteria plus rational, emotional and social motivations.

### Current persona state

Best working role cluster:
- Food Services / Dining Operations Manager;
- food engineer / technical food-services professional;
- contractor production/operations lead where contractor owns quantity risk.

Boğaziçi provides a real public governance anchor through its Food Services Branch Manager, but public sources do **not** establish who owns the daily production quantity. Therefore the named person can be used as a PMR starting point, not yet as a fully validated end-user persona.

### Persona facts that PMR must obtain

- actual decision owned;
- decision frequency and freeze time;
- what failure looks like personally/organizationally;
- current workaround;
- key performance metric;
- who complains/escalates when food runs out;
- who notices or pays when food is overproduced;
- data/tools used today;
- trust requirements;
- purchasing criteria in rank order;
- who can veto adoption;
- economic buyer vs end user vs champion.

### Candidate purchasing-criteria hypothesis

Do not submit this as validated. Test the likely ordering:
1. service continuity / avoid shortages;
2. operational reliability and food-safety compatibility;
3. recommendation available before freeze point;
4. low workflow friction;
5. explainability and human control;
6. measurable surplus/waste improvement;
7. integration/data burden;
8. cost/procurement burden.

## 6. PMR method implications

DE Step 1A makes direct prospective-customer interaction central. The highest-value interview is not `Would you use AI?`; it reconstructs a real incident.

Use:

```text
last concrete mismatch
→ information available
→ who made the decision
→ decision time
→ freeze point
→ quantity / buffer
→ operational constraint
→ result
→ surplus/shortage consequence
→ what was measured
→ what changed next time
```

### Falsification first

The current dining wedge should be modified/killed if repeated PMR shows:
- no reachable/changeable production decision;
- demand mismatch is not a material cause of avoidable surplus/waste;
- shortage risk makes quantity reduction operationally unacceptable;
- the relevant actor captures no value and has no incentive to change;
- service-level outcomes cannot be measured at acceptable effort;
- each institution requires a fundamentally different product and sales process.

## 7. Application-copy implications

### Problem
Keep the problem at the customer/control-point level, not `campus sustainability is inefficient`.

Safe structure:
> Dining teams must commit production before actual demand is known. When planning information is incomplete or fragmented, the operation can experience surplus or shortages. We are testing how material and actionable this mismatch is in the target workflow.

### Beachhead
Do not write `all universities` or `large universities` alone. Include workflow qualifiers.

### Persona
Use a real anchor only for verified public background. Mark unverified motivations/purchasing criteria as assumptions to test.

### PMR
Application value should come from what changed in the team's beliefs, not interview count or positive reactions.

## 8. Expansion discipline

DE follow-on-market logic recommends changing one variable at a time.

A defensible sequence is more like:

1. same dining product → second university with similar workflow;
2. same product → another institutional kitchen segment;
3. same university buyer / governance network → adjacent operational decision product;
4. only later broader campus resource decision platform.

Jumping directly from dining forecasting to shuttle + rooms + energy + water means changing both product and decision owner at once and weakens the beachhead logic.

## 9. Bottom line

The strongest DE-consistent BOUNCAMPUS narrative is:

```text
large climate/sustainability vision
→ one narrow repeated operational decision
→ one real decision owner
→ one homogeneous workflow segment
→ falsifiable PMR
→ bounded pilot
→ replicate in same segment
→ expand one variable at a time
```

The current repository is technically ahead of its market evidence. The next selection advantage comes from PMR quality and segment precision, not adding more modules.
