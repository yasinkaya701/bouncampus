# Disciplined Entrepreneurship Alignment — 2026-10-04

**Status:** Methodology alignment based on Disciplined Entrepreneurship primary sources. This document changes how we structure market research; it does **not** convert secondary research into customer validation.

## Executive conclusion

The current BOUNCAMPUS direction is broadly compatible with Disciplined Entrepreneurship, but four gates must be enforced more strictly:

1. **Beachhead homogeneity:** the initial segment must share the same product, same sales process/value proposition, and strong community/word-of-mouth links.
2. **Persona specificity:** the Persona must ultimately be a real individual, not a generic title or composite role.
3. **DMU separation:** end user, champion, primary economic buyer, influencers, and veto holders must be mapped separately even when one person occupies multiple roles.
4. **Key assumptions:** hypotheses must be narrow enough to be independently falsifiable rather than bundling several beliefs into one interview question.

# 1. Beachhead: current segment passes the problem test but has not yet passed the market test

Disciplined Entrepreneurship Step 2 defines three core conditions for a market:

1. end users use the **same product**;
2. the **same sales process** and substantially interchangeable value proposition can be used;
3. there is meaningful **word of mouth/community connection** between end users.

The methodology also recommends evaluating target segments on customer funding, accessibility, compelling reason to buy, ability to deliver a whole product, entrenched competition, leverage into adjacent markets, team fit, and speed to win.

## Implication for BOUNCAMPUS

The current phrase:

> medium-to-large Turkish universities with centrally coordinated or institution-governed dining operations

is a useful screening frame, but is not yet sufficiently proven as a single market.

Why:

- university-operated quantity decisions and contractor-operated quantity decisions can have different buyers and value capture;
- served-count settlement versus fixed/requested quantity settlement changes economics;
- reservation-based and walk-in-heavy operations may require materially different workflows;
- public and foundation universities may have very different procurement paths;
- a specialist incumbent may change both product scope and sales process.

## Required beachhead certification tests

For at least 3 institutions in the proposed segment, PMR must verify:

```text
same core user job
same first-use product
same reason to act now
same/similar pilot approval path
same/similar purchasing route
same/similar buyer type
similar integration burden
credible peer/reference network
```

If the user job repeats but the buying process does not, treat these as separate market segments even if both are called universities.

## Word-of-mouth test

This is currently weakly evidenced.

PMR should ask:

- Which peer universities or professional groups do dining managers benchmark?
- Which procurement/specification precedents do teams reuse?
- Which SKS/facilities/food-engineering networks exchange practices?
- Who would the interviewee call to ask how another university handles meal planning or waste?

A segment with no practical peer-reference channel may still be a customer category but fails one of the DE market-homogeneity conditions.

# 2. Beachhead selection scorecard adapted to BOUNCAMPUS

Use the DE eight criteria as qualitative gates, not invented numerical precision.

| DE criterion | BOUNCAMPUS interpretation | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| well funded | budget or operational value owner exists | UNKNOWN |
| accessible | team can reach real operations/contractor decision roles | TESTING |
| compelling reason | repeated surplus/shortage or reporting/operational pain | TESTING |
| whole product | can deliver recommendation + measurement + integration needed for pilot | TESTING |
| competition | incumbent does not already solve exact decision sufficiently | TESTING |
| adjacent leverage | same operating contract transfers to other kitchens/campus decisions | PLAUSIBLE, not validated |
| team fit | team can execute data/model/hardware/operations discovery | internal capability question |
| speed to win | bounded pilot can occur without full procurement replacement | UNKNOWN |

### Kill condition

Do not retain a beachhead simply because the problem is interesting. If pilot access, sales process, economics or whole-product burden make a different segment materially easier to win, re-segment.

# 3. Persona: a role family is not yet a DE Persona

Step 5 states that the Persona should be a **specific real person**, with detailed needs, behaviors, motivations and a prioritized list of purchasing criteria. Rational, emotional and social motivations matter.

Current secondary research has identified a useful **persona family**:

> operational owner of high-volume institutional meal production/allocation under uncertain demand

Possible titles include Food Services Branch Manager, Cafeteria Manager/Director, food engineer with planning responsibility, contractor project manager, or central-kitchen production manager.

That is still only an End User Profile / discovery target.

## Promotion gate to a true Persona

The repository should not call a specific named person the Persona until PMR verifies that the person:

- directly experiences or owns the relevant production/allocation decision;
- can describe recent decision failures;
- has meaningful influence over action;
- has stable goals/constraints representative of the proposed segment;
- can rank tradeoffs rather than merely endorse them.

If the real quantity owner is contractor-side, the Persona should move there rather than forcing a university staff member into the role.

# 4. Purchasing criteria must be behaviorally ranked

Current working priority hypothesis:

1. service continuity / avoid unacceptable stockouts;
2. recommendation timing before freeze point;
3. low operational burden;
4. reliability / graceful behavior with missing data;
5. explainability and operator control;
6. compatibility with current contractor/data systems;
7. measurable waste/cost improvement;
8. privacy/security/procurement burden;
9. sustainability/reporting benefit.

This ordering is **not validated**.

## Better interview technique

Do not ask:

> Rank these nine features from 1 to 9.

First reconstruct actual tradeoffs:

- Tell me about the last time you intentionally produced extra food. Why?
- What would have been worse than the surplus?
- When have you ignored a data-based estimate because operations felt riskier?
- What new tool or process have you rejected recently? Why?
- Which failure would create the most complaints/escalation for you?
- If a recommendation saves food but creates one extra early sell-out, is that acceptable? What determines the answer?

Then ask for explicit ranking only after behavioral evidence.

# 5. Emotional and social motivations to test

DE Step 5 warns against limiting the Persona to rational criteria.

Candidate non-economic motivations in this workflow should be tested rather than assumed:

- avoiding visible service failure and student complaints;
- avoiding blame/escalation after a shortage;
- professional confidence in a high-volume service;
- desire to preserve autonomy versus an opaque algorithm;
- reluctance to introduce staff burden;
- credibility with university administration or contractor management;
- ability to demonstrate measured sustainability improvements.

These can explain adoption resistance better than a generic ROI model.

# 6. DMU: primary Persona is not automatically the buyer

Disciplined Entrepreneurship Step 12 separates:

- **End User** — uses the product;
- **Champion** — wants the organization to adopt it;
- **Primary Economic Buyer** — has authority over the money;
- **Influencers** — shape the decision;
- **Veto holders** — can stop adoption.

For BOUNCAMPUS the working DMU is:

| DMU role | Working hypothesis | Must be validated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End User | production planner / food engineer / kitchen or operations manager | who reads/acts on recommendation |
| Champion | operator with pain, sustainability owner, or manager seeking measurable improvement | who pushes pilot internally |
| Economic Buyer | university/SKS/facilities or contractor depending contract | who owns budget/value capture |
| Technical influencer | IT/data owner, food engineer, analytics/operations | integration and trust criteria |
| Veto | procurement, privacy/security, food safety, senior administration, contractor terms | what can stop even a desired pilot |

## Required PMR questions

- What would we need to do to run a small test here?
- Who besides you needs to agree?
- Who controls the relevant budget?
- Who could stop the pilot even if the operator wants it?
- Who is accountable if meal availability worsens?
- Who captures the savings if unnecessary production falls?

A technically enthusiastic food engineer may be a strong champion but not the economic buyer.

# 7. PMR: secondary research must stay separate

Step 1a distinguishes primary research from secondary research. Secondary/public evidence can establish context, mechanisms, comparable workflows and interview targets, but not customer-specific validation.

Therefore:

- public procurement clauses do not validate willingness to buy;
- public organization charts do not validate decision ownership;
- academic forecasting studies do not validate workflow fit;
- official waste totals do not validate the causal source of waste;
- competitor websites do not validate a current-tool gap at Boğaziçi.

Only completed direct interactions with relevant potential customers/stakeholders can produce `INTERVIEW EVIDENCE` in the KREATE registry.

---

# 8. Interview behavior: remain in inquiry mode

Step 7 explicitly warns technical teams against moving too early from inquiry mode into advocacy/feature design. Step 1a points to The Mom Test principle: do not ask people whether the business idea is good.

Interview order should therefore be:

```text
real incident
-> current process
-> constraints
-> existing alternative
-> consequence
-> authority / DMU
-> only later reaction to a high-level concept
```

Do not begin by showing TrayGate, the forecast UI, AI claims or sustainability dashboards.

Any product reaction should occur after the interviewee's independent workflow has been captured.

# 9. Key assumptions: split compound beliefs

Step 20 requires narrow, independently testable assumptions.

The current three application hypotheses are useful for the form, but internally they should be decomposed further:

## Control-point family

- KA-01: actual demand is not fully known at the first production commitment.
- KA-02: a named role owns or approves quantity.
- KA-03: quantity remains adjustable until a discoverable freeze point.
- KA-04: information exists before the freeze point that could change the decision.

## Problem family

- KA-05: meaningful production-demand mismatches recur.
- KA-06: a material fraction of avoidable surplus is caused upstream of service by quantity planning.
- KA-07: shortage/early sell-out is a meaningful counter-risk.

## Status-quo family

- KA-08: current planning uses fragmented signals or heuristics with a material residual gap.
- KA-09: current specialist tools do not already solve the relevant decision sufficiently.

## Adoption/economics family

- KA-10: an end user can incorporate a recommendation without excessive friction.
- KA-11: a reachable champion exists.
- KA-12: a reachable economic buyer captures enough value to justify adoption.
- KA-13: privacy, procurement, integration and food-safety vetoes can be satisfied in a bounded pilot.

## Measurement family

- KA-14: actual served demand can be measured consistently at service level.
- KA-15: surplus/waste can be measured at a decision-relevant stage.
- KA-16: matched pilot services can be collected without major measurement bias.

The application may still present only three hypotheses, but the research program should track these component assumptions independently.

# 10. What should change in the application narrative

## Keep

- narrow dining beachhead;
- production decision as the first named decision;
- human-reviewed recommendation;
- verification loop;
- explicit unknowns and falsification.

## Tighten

- describe the beachhead as a **candidate segment under PMR**, not validated market;
- make the primary Persona the real quantity-decision actor once identified;
- distinguish end user from economic buyer/champion;
- describe purchasing criteria as hypotheses until ranked by real behavior;
- avoid implying all large universities have the same sales process.

## Avoid

- `all universities`;
- `sustainability managers` as generic primary users;
- assuming the public institution is the payer when contractor economics may dominate;
- using a named public staff member as the Persona without confirming actual decision ownership;
- product advocacy before workflow discovery.

# 11. Highest-value next PMR output

The single most valuable interview artifact remains:

```text
real person
-> last production mismatch
-> signals used
-> quantity owner
-> freeze point
-> actual action
-> surplus/shortage consequence
-> current workaround
-> purchasing priority
-> champion
-> economic buyer
-> veto path
-> referral to a peer institution
```

If this sequence repeats across multiple institutions with substantially the same product and sales process, the beachhead becomes much more defensible under Disciplined Entrepreneurship.
