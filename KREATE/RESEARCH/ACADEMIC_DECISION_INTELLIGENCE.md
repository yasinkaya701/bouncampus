# Academic Evidence — From Monitoring to Decision Intelligence

**Research date:** 2026-10-01  
**Status:** Peer-reviewed secondary research. External results are design references, not Boğaziçi outcomes.

## Core conclusion

The literature supports three things that are useful for BOUNCAMPUS:

1. university food waste is multi-causal and must be measured at sufficient granularity;
2. forecasting and structural/service interventions can reduce waste in some institutional settings, but reported effects are context-specific;
3. smart-campus value appears when data is connected to actual organizational decision processes, not when sensing/dashboarding exists by itself.

It does **not** establish that demand mismatch is the dominant cause of Boğaziçi's 48,251 kg reported 2025 food waste.

# A-01 — Educational food-waste literature says granular causes and operational strategies matter

Kaur, Dhir, Talwar & Alrasheedy (2021) systematically reviewed **88 studies** on food waste in educational institutions. The synthesis organizes evidence around drivers, quantitative assessment, behavioral aspects, operational reduction strategies, behavior-change interventions, diversion/disposal and implementation barriers.

Paper:
https://consensus.app/papers/systematic-literature-review-of-food-waste-in-educational-kaur-dhir/41d44cdd08b7512597ef87b8b9fe942d/?utm_source=chatgpt

Metadata: 2021, *International Journal of Contemporary Hospitality Management*, 91 citations at research date.

**Agent implication:** do not model a university-wide monthly waste total as if it were one causal stream. Separate production surplus, preparation loss and plate waste where operationally feasible.

# A-02 — University food-waste interventions are heterogeneous

Leal et al. (2023) review university food-waste practices and report broad variation across institutions and intervention types. Their synthesis groups priority action areas around planning/awareness, preparation/storage, services and direct waste reuse. The review reports observed reduction ranges across studies, but those values should be treated as external benchmarks rather than transferable effect sizes.

Paper:
https://consensus.app/papers/toward-food-waste-reduction-at-universities-leal-ribeiro/ccb8fe652ec75ed58b6b030afe3423d6/?utm_source=chatgpt

Metadata: 2023, *Environment, Development and Sustainability*, 57 citations at research date.

**Agent implication:** BOUNCAMPUS should diagnose which waste stage is addressable before selecting the intervention. Forecasting is only one intervention family.

# A-03 — A Türkiye-relevant campus-demand paper supports the technical problem class, not our impact claim

Gül Fatma Türker (2025) studied campus dining demand using variables including meal variety/counts, revenue, campus mobility and temperature. The study compares multiple regression/tree/boosting methods and reports strong prediction performance for some models; its abstract also reports a food-waste outcome associated with balancing production and demand.

Paper:
https://consensus.app/papers/reducing-food-waste-in-campus-dining-a-datadriven-approach-turker/f9cb99983e0859509e84b8549e19c9f5/?utm_source=chatgpt

Metadata: 2025, *Sustainability*, 14 citations at research date.

**Caution:** the abstract wording around the "28%" waste result should not be reused as a Boğaziçi reduction claim, and the exact intervention/evaluation design should be checked in the full paper before using that percentage in application copy.

**CS1 implication:** candidate features such as mobility, calendar/context and menu are reasonable to test, but the baseline should remain simple until real Boğaziçi data shows incremental value.

# A-05 — Türkiye has a campus-scale circular food-waste benchmark at İTÜ

Maçin, Arıkan, Altınbaş & Damgaard (2023) analyze food-waste management at ITU Ayazağa Campus. The case uses **577 tonnes/year of separated food waste** and compares anaerobic digestion, composting, incineration and landfill through life-cycle assessment and business-model analysis. The paper highlights anaerobic digestion for circularity/low toxicity in the selected comparison.

Paper:
https://consensus.app/papers/decarbonizing-university-campuses-a-business-model-for-maçin-arıkan/6d18bdac4fb55618b4f9a276358c1dc7/?utm_source=chatgpt

Metadata: 2023, *Environmental Progress & Sustainable Energy*, 9 citations at research date.

**Product implication:** keep upstream prevention and downstream treatment conceptually separate. BOUNCAMPUS's food wedge is strongest if it prevents avoidable production surplus; circular recovery handles the residual stream.

# A-06 — Smart-campus research warns that data does not automatically improve decisions

Valks, Arkesteijn, Koutamanis & den Heijer (2020) examine smart-campus IoT and organizational decision processes. Their abstract explicitly notes that literature suggests IoT information is often not used in organizational decision-making and analyzes process requirements for connecting data to campus-management decisions.

Paper:
https://consensus.app/papers/towards-a-smart-campus-supporting-campus-decisions-with-valks-arkesteijn/b023f515363f52ec9e289cf4f57d6659/?utm_source=chatgpt

Metadata: 2020, *Building Research & Information*, volume 49, 74 citations at research date.

**Central BOUNCAMPUS implication:** "more sensors" is not a sufficient product thesis. Start with a named decision, owner, timing, action space and feedback loop.

# A-08 — Waste-reduction research warns against over-indexing on awareness

Wilson, Delmas & Rajagopal (2025) synthesize **99 experimental and quasi-experimental studies** across wasteful resource behaviors. They identify information/feedback, social norms, economic incentives, choice architecture, goal setting and emotional appeals, but argue that individual-level focus can overlook structural/system-wide changes and call for scalable measurement tools.

Paper:
https://consensus.app/papers/behavioral-interventions-for-waste-reduction-a-wilson-delmas/57aad5b537a25524ab863a27013a0f6c/?utm_source=chatgpt

Metadata: 2025, *Frontiers in Psychology*, volume 16, 24 citations at research date.

**Product implication:** a student eco-points/awareness app should not be the core wedge unless PMR shows the operational problem is primarily behavioral and student-controlled.

# CS1 modeling rules derived from research

1. Establish a naive operational baseline before complex ML: same weekday/meal moving average or comparable historical mean.
2. Optimize **decision loss**, not only prediction error. Overproduction and shortage may have asymmetric costs.
3. Use uncertainty explicitly and preserve human override.
4. Evaluate features incrementally; menu/weather/mobility/calendar should stay only if they improve out-of-sample decision utility.
5. Avoid leakage from post-service variables.
6. Segment by campus/meal/service regime when data volume supports it.
7. Report calibration separately from point-prediction accuracy.
8. A model that predicts demand accurately but cannot change a production decision has low product value.
9. Record reasons for operator overrides; they may expose missing variables and constraints.
10. Treat intervention impact as a separate causal question from forecasting accuracy.

---

# EE measurement rules derived from research

1. Directly weigh waste if operationally feasible; record measurement method.
2. Separate preparation waste, production surplus and plate waste where possible.
3. Track served portions so waste can be normalized by service volume.
4. Measure shortage guardrails such as early sell-outs/substitutions.
5. Keep food-safety constraints outside model optimization unless formally encoded and reviewed.
6. Prefer a small reliable dataset over a large mixed-semantics dataset.

---

# IE / PMR rules derived from research

Do not ask stakeholders whether forecasting sounds useful. Ask for the last concrete production error and reconstruct:

```text
trigger -> information available -> person -> decision time -> quantity -> constraint -> outcome -> waste/shortage -> correction
```

The key falsification question is:

> If we had predicted demand better at the relevant time, could the operator legally and practically have changed production enough to affect the outcome?

If the answer is consistently no, the current control point is wrong even if forecasting research is strong.

# CS2 application rules derived from research

Safe statement:

> Academic research shows that demand forecasting and operational interventions can reduce food waste in some institutional settings, while smart-campus research emphasizes linking data to real management decisions. We are testing whether that mechanism exists in Boğaziçi dining operations.

Unsafe statement:

> Research proves BOUNCAMPUS will reduce Boğaziçi food waste by X%.
