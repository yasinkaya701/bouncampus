# Persona Role-Boundary Evidence — University Dining Operations

**Research date:** 2026-10-05
**Status:** Official/public role research. This establishes formal responsibilities, not unobserved motivations or daily decision ownership.

## 1. Boğaziçi Food Services Branch — formal authority is substantial

Official Boğaziçi SKS directive PDF:
https://mediastore.cc.bogazici.edu.tr/web/upload/sayfalar/1526-saglik-kultur-ve-spor-daire-baskanligi-20260227-161845.pdf

The directive lists Food Services Branch responsibilities including:
- preparation work for food-service tenders;
- preparing technical specifications for dining operations to be tendered;
- following and controlling the food service provided by the contractor;
- proposing/implementing food-service prices under relevant rules;
- preparing and publishing student/personnel menus;
- controlling compliance of the service with contract and technical specification;
- secretariat for the Food Services Executive Board.

Current public staff page:
https://yemekhane.bogazici.edu.tr/people

It lists a named Food Services Branch Manager plus office/operations staff.

### What this supports

A Food Services Branch Manager is a credible **institutional governance / operational champion / decision-unit member** for PMR because the role owns major parts of:
- contractor governance;
- specification;
- menu/service policy;
- compliance;
- pricing/service administration.

### What it does NOT support

The formal directive does not state:
- who chooses tomorrow's number of portions;
- who makes the contractor's production forecast;
- exact decision freeze time;
- who bears excess-production cost;
- who can approve a production reduction on the day;
- who owns the forecasting software budget.

Therefore do not equate `Branch Manager` automatically with `daily production-quantity end user`.

## 2. ITU confirms a repeatable food-operations management role cluster

Official ITU administrative structure:
https://sks.itu.edu.tr/hakkimizda/i-dari-yap%C4%B1

Current ITU structure includes a dedicated **Yemek İşletmeleri Şube Müdürlüğü / dining manager**.

Current food-services page:
https://sks.itu.edu.tr/hizmetlerimiz/beslenme-hizmetleri

It describes:
- multiple dining halls;
- legal/food-safety obligations;
- continuous improvement based on user requests;
- card/automation usage;
- large daily service volume.

ITU also publishes formal process inventories for Food Operations including:
- technical service;
- POS/kiosk;
- food-service inspection/control;
- goods/material acceptance.

Source:
https://sks.itu.edu.tr/hakkimizda/is-akis-surecleri-2025

### Implication

A dedicated food-operations management/governance role repeats beyond Boğaziçi, supporting the general persona **role family**.

## 3. Technical food-domain roles are separate and important

Across university food-service organizations/public documents, recurring technical roles include:
- food engineer;
- dietitian;
- food technician;
- head chef;
- quality/food-safety staff.

Example Hacettepe technical specification signatures publicly show dietitians and a food engineer participating in food technical-specification work.

Source:
https://sksdb.hacettepe.edu.tr/SatinalmaDuyurulari/Kuru-Gida-Teknik-Sartname-pdf-132863607554459645.pdf

### Persona implication

The product's daily user, technical validator and economic buyer may be different people.

A likely Decision Making Unit (DMU) hypothesis is:

```text
institutional governance/champion:
    Food Services / Dining Operations Manager

technical influencer/user:
    food engineer / production planner / kitchen lead

execution user:
    contractor or kitchen production operator

economic buyer:
    UNKNOWN — institution or contractor depending operating model

veto / control:
    procurement + food safety + IT/data + contract owner
```

## 4. Persona form — what is fact vs assumption

### Publicly supportable background

Safe:
- role is university food-service operations/governance;
- responsibilities can include contractor oversight, specifications, menu/service administration and compliance;
- role operates within SKS/administrative governance;
- role works with technical food-service staff and/or contractor.

### PMR assumptions only

Do not state as facts until interviewed:
- age;
- frustration with spreadsheets;
- fear of shortages;
- intentional safety buffer;
- desire for AI;
- willingness to pay;
- personal sustainability motivation;
- exact KPI priority;
- preferred interface;
- distrust of black-box recommendations;
- daily quantity ownership.

These are hypotheses to validate.

## 5. Stronger persona architecture

Instead of forcing one fictional person to own everything, distinguish:

### Primary end-user hypothesis
**Production Quantity Decision Owner**
- exact title unknown until PMR;
- may be contractor-side production lead, food engineer or university-side operator depending contract.

### Institutional champion hypothesis
**Food Services / Dining Operations Branch Manager**
- has governance relevance;
- can route the team to real operator/data/contract owner;
- may help approve a pilot.

### Technical influencer
**Food Engineer / Food Safety / Kitchen Planner**
- validates portion/menu/food-safety/measurement assumptions;
- likely knows real production process in detail.

### Economic buyer
**UNKNOWN**
- may be SKS/university administration;
- may be contractor HQ;
- may require joint approval.

## 6. Persona PMR sequence

The first institutional interview should not try to force the branch manager into the end-user persona.

Ask:
1. Walk us through yesterday/tomorrow's production quantity process.
2. Who actually enters/chooses the number?
3. Who approves it?
4. Who at the contractor owns the production plan?
5. Which role handles shortage/excess incidents?
6. Who owns the relevant operational data?
7. Who would have to approve a pilot?
8. Who benefits economically if surplus decreases?

Then update the persona based on the referral chain.

## 7. Prioritized purchasing criteria — still hypotheses

Working order to test, not submission fact:
1. no degradation in service continuity;
2. food safety / contractual compliance;
3. recommendation before freeze point;
4. operational reliability;
5. low staff burden;
6. understandable reason/risk;
7. measurable outcome;
8. integration compatibility;
9. cost/procurement effort.

Ask interviewees to rank or reveal these through concrete past decisions rather than accepting this order.

## 8. Strong application wording

Safe:
> `Our working persona is the operational role that can change or strongly influence meal-production quantity. Public organizational research identifies university Food Services managers and technical food-service staff as key members of that decision unit; PMR will identify the exact daily decision owner.`

Weaker / overclaimed:
> `The Food Services Branch Manager decides tomorrow's production quantity.`

Public sources currently do not establish that.

## 9. Bottom line

Secondary evidence supports a **real, repeatable organizational role family**, but not yet a single validated end-user persona.

That distinction is valuable: the team's next interview objective is not to ask whether a manager likes BOUNCAMPUS; it is to use the manager's formal governance position to map the real daily decision owner, technical influencer, economic buyer and veto chain.
