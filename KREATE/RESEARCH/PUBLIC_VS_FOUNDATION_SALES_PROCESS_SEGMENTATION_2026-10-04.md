# Public vs Foundation University Sales-Process Segmentation — 2026-10-04

**Purpose:** test the Disciplined Entrepreneurship `same sales process` criterion across public and foundation universities.  
**Status:** legal/process secondary research. **Not legal advice and not proof that one segment buys faster or more easily.**

## Executive conclusion

Public and foundation universities should **not** currently be assumed to share the same sales/procurement process.

The legal and organizational purchasing frameworks differ materially:

- public universities operate inside the public-procurement framework applicable to public administrations;
- foundation higher-education institutions are explicitly excluded from one part of the 4734 scope and operate under the separate **Vakıf Yükseköğretim Kurumları İhale Yönetmeliği** plus institution-specific procurement structures/procedures.

Therefore:

```text
same dining pain
!=
same beachhead automatically
```

The same product can still eventually serve both, but DE beachhead validation should test public and foundation GTM separately.

---

# 1. Public-procurement scope distinguishes foundation higher education

The current consolidated text of **4734 Sayılı Kamu İhale Kanunu**, Article 2, defines the public entities covered by the law and explicitly contains an exclusion for `vakıf yüksek öğretim kurumları` in the relevant public-legal-person category.

Current legal-text source:
https://www.lexpera.com.tr/mevzuat/kanunlar/kamu-ihale-kanunu-4734/69

Kamu İhale Kurumu official legislation portal:
https://www.ihale.gov.tr/Mevzuat.aspx?AnaSayfa=true

### Implication

Do not assume a foundation university follows the same EKAP / 4734 path as Boğaziçi or another public university for an equivalent purchase.

---

# 2. Foundation universities have a dedicated YÖK procurement regulation

The **Vakıf Yükseköğretim Kurumları İhale Yönetmeliği** was published in the Official Gazette on **16 November 2018, No. 30597** and regulates foundation-university:

- goods and service purchases/sales;
- construction;
- real-estate transactions;
- other procurement activities.

The regulation's definition of services explicitly covers categories including:

- food preparation/distribution;
- consulting/research;
- computer systems;
- software services.

Legal-text source:
https://www.lexpera.com.tr/resmi-gazete/metin/vakif-yuksekogretim-kurumlari-ihale-yonetmeligi-30597

Current consolidated regulation metadata shows a last amendment in 2024:
https://www.lexpera.com.tr/mevzuat/yonetmelikler/vakif-yuksekogretim-kurumlari-ihale-yonetmeligi

### Strategic implication

A software/analytics procurement at a foundation university has a different legal/process basis from a public-university procurement.

This does **not** by itself prove it is faster, slower, easier or harder.

---

# 3. Foundation institutions can have their own detailed procurement procedures

For example, İstanbul Sabahattin Zaim University publishes an institution-specific purchasing/tender regulation explicitly based on the Foundation Higher Education Institutions Tender Regulation.

Official university source:
https://izu.edu.tr/izu-hakkinda/mevzuat/yonetmelikler/sat%C4%B1n-alma-ve-i-hale-y%C3%B6netmeli%C4%9Fi

Other foundation universities similarly publish institution-specific procurement regulations.

### Beachhead implication

`foundation university` is itself not necessarily one uniform sales process.

PMR must still identify:

- request owner;
- budget owner;
- procurement office;
- approval thresholds;
- pilot route;
- IT/security review;
- contract form.

# 5. Why `foundation = easy pilot` is an unsafe assumption

Foundation institutions may avoid some public-university procurement mechanics, but they can still have:

- formal procurement rules;
- internal tender instructions;
- multi-level approvals;
- finance controls;
- IT/security review;
- vendor onboarding;
- operational contracts with catering providers.

Do not say:

> We will start with foundation universities because they can buy software immediately.

No current evidence supports that generalization.

---

# 6. Public-university route remains structurally different

For a public university such as Boğaziçi, current research must consider:

- 4734/related public-procurement processes;
- EKAP and public tender/direct-procurement rules where applicable;
- annual monetary-limit controls;
- formal contract/specification governance;
- an active catering-service contract that may constrain operational changes.

See:
`BUSINESS_MODEL_AND_PUBLIC_PROCUREMENT_ENTRY_2026-10-04.md`.

### Consequence

Even if end-user pain and technical solution are identical, the sales/deployment path can differ enough to violate DE beachhead homogeneity.

# 7. PMR sampling rule

Do not combine public + foundation interviews and conclude `five universities confirmed the same market` unless the buying path also matches.

Record for each institution:

```text
institution_type
requesting_unit
end_user
operational_owner
budget_owner
procurement_unit
approval_chain
pilot_authority
IT/security review
contractor involvement
expected purchase method
sales-cycle estimate from stakeholder
```

Then compare.

# 8. Possible segmentation outcomes

## Outcome A — same product, different sales process

Then:
- same technical market;
- separate GTM segment/beachhead sequence.

Example:
```text
public universities first
foundation later
```

or vice versa based on validated access/value.

## Outcome B — same product and materially similar sales process

Then the segments may be combined after evidence shows the difference is operationally minor.

## Outcome C — product also changes

If foundation institutions use different dining/operator/data architectures, they may become a genuinely adjacent market rather than just a different procurement route.

# 9. Bilkent PMR questions derived from this research

To Cafeterias Management:

- Who can approve a no-cost bounded pilot?
- What changes for a paid deployment?
- Does Cafeterias Management hold its own budget?
- When does Procurement Office enter the process?
- Which other units review operational software/data integration?
- Is catering vendor approval required?
- What documentation is needed from a new vendor?

To Procurement / Secretary General route if referred:

- What is the normal process for a small operational software pilot?
- Are recurring SaaS subscriptions treated differently from implementation/consulting?
- What approval/tender path applies at different spend/risk levels?
- Does a successful unit pilot simplify later purchase?

---

# 10. Application consequence

Safe:

> We are initially segmenting universities by operating and purchasing workflow rather than treating all universities as one market. Public and foundation institutions can share the same dining problem while following different procurement and approval processes.

Unsafe:

> All Turkish universities have the same sales process.

Unsafe:

> Foundation universities are faster/easier customers.

Both require PMR.

---

## Current conclusion

For Disciplined Entrepreneurship, `same product` is not enough.

The current research suggests the initial beachhead may need to be narrower than:

> medium-to-large Turkish universities.

A stronger eventual segment could look like:

> **high-volume public universities with contractor/coordinated dining and a specific decision/payment architecture**

or

> **foundation universities with dedicated cafeteria management and a repeatable internal procurement path**.

Which is better remains a PMR/GTM question, not a secondary-research conclusion.
