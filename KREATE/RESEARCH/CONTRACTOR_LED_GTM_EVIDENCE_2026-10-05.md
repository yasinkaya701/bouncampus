# Contractor-Led GTM Evidence — Multi-Site Catering Operators

**Research date:** 2026-10-05
**Status:** Secondary/public research. This supports a go-to-market hypothesis; it does not establish software buying authority, willingness to pay, or product-market fit.

## Research question

Could BOUNCAMPUS reach a more homogeneous and scalable initial market through a multi-site catering operator rather than selling university-by-university?

## 1. TEMAŞ operating scale

Official TEMAŞ website:
- https://www.temasgida.com/
- https://www.temasgida.com/hakkimizda

Current public claims include:
- service at **65 different locations**;
- **2,000+ employees**;
- **200+ on-site production** operations;
- **500+ transported-meal** operations;
- 30+ years of sector experience;
- customer-specific kitchens / menu alternatives.

These are vendor-reported scale claims, not independently audited operational statistics.

### Implication

A single contractor may operate across many sites with repeated production-planning processes. If the same forecasting/production problem exists across its contracts, contractor-level adoption could provide much higher rollout leverage than one university at a time.

## 3. Why contractor-led beachhead might be stronger

### Repeated operational problem

A multi-site caterer can face the same core decision repeatedly:

```text
site-specific expected demand
→ production quantity / batch plan
→ ingredient + labor commitment
→ service
→ excess / shortage / emergency replenishment
→ settlement / margin
```

### Potential benefits

If quantity risk sits with the contractor, better decisions can potentially affect:
- ingredient waste;
- emergency production / labor;
- shortage penalties or client complaints;
- gross margin;
- cross-site operational consistency;
- tender/pricing confidence;
- evidence used in future bids.

### Rollout leverage

A contractor relationship may enable:

```text
one buyer / operations organization
→ multiple university or institutional sites
```

This could satisfy Disciplined Entrepreneurship's leverage/speed criteria better than independent university sales — **if** the buyer, workflow and integration are genuinely shared.

## 4. Why contractor-led may still be worse

Do not assume contractor-led is automatically better.

Possible blockers:
- local project managers may not control software procurement;
- contractor HQ may already have ERP/planning/BI tools;
- client data may be owned by the university/institution;
- academic-calendar / campus-context data may be harder for contractor systems to access;
- each public contract may impose different settlement and service rules;
- adoption may require client approval even if contractor pays;
- tender margins may not justify new software/integration burden;
- contractor may consider demand heuristics proprietary operational know-how.

## 5. Direct comparison: university-led vs contractor-led GTM

| Dimension | University-led | Contractor-led |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End user | Food Services / SKS / kitchen | contractor production/project ops |
| Economic buyer | university or unclear | contractor HQ/ops or unclear |
| Rollout | one institution | potentially many sites |
| Procurement burden | public-university procurement | internal vendor procurement + possible client approval |
| Data access | university systems may be easier | cross-client data ownership can be harder |
| Financial pain | may be indirect under outsourced contract | potentially direct if contractor bears excess/shortage risk |
| Campus context | naturally available institution-side | must integrate/receive from client |
| Reference network | university peer network | contractor's own multi-site organization |
| Homogeneity | varies by university | may improve if contractor standardizes operations |

## 6. Highest-value contractor PMR questions

Target:
- TEMAŞ HQ operations / production planning;
- TEMAŞ Boğaziçi local project lead;
- another TEMAŞ university/institution site;
- procurement/IT/ERP owner at contractor HQ.

Ask:
1. Who sets daily meal quantities at each client site?
2. Is the process standardized across sites or client-specific?
3. Which ERP/planning tool is used centrally?
4. Does HQ receive planned, produced, served and waste data from sites?
5. How frequently are site forecasts overridden?
6. Who bears the cost of overproduction at each contract?
7. How are shortage penalties/emergency substitutions handled?
8. Could one forecasting/decision tool be deployed across sites or would client-specific integrations dominate?
9. Who at HQ can approve a software pilot?
10. Which client data require client approval to access?
11. Does improved forecasting affect contract margin materially?
12. Are production heuristics already considered a competitive capability internally?
13. Would a pilot at one university create a credible internal reference for other sites?

## 7. Strong falsification criteria

Reject contractor-led as the first GTM if interviews show:
- quantity decisions are fully dictated by each client and contractor cannot influence them;
- software/data workflows differ so much that no common product exists;
- HQ has no centralized decision/data layer and local sites cannot buy;
- client data permissions make cross-site use impractical;
- current ERP already adequately solves the decision;
- demand mismatch has negligible margin/service impact for contractor.

## 8. Strategic possibility

There may be a stronger sequence than `university-by-university SaaS`:

```text
Boğaziçi pilot
→ validate one real contractor/university workflow
→ contractor HQ evidence review
→ repeat at second contractor-operated site
→ test whether one integration/product can scale across portfolio
```

If successful, the first scalable customer could be **the catering operator**, while universities remain deployment sites / institutional gatekeepers.

## 9. What public research now supports

Safe:
> `Large catering operators can serve many institutional sites, creating a plausible multi-site rollout path if the quantity decision and data workflow are standardized.`

Safe:
> `TEMAŞ publicly reports a large multi-site operation and has a substantial public-procurement history across many administrations.`

Still hypothesis:
> `TEMAŞ is a better buyer than Boğaziçi.`

Still hypothesis:
> `One BOUNCAMPUS integration can scale across TEMAŞ sites.`

Still unknown:
> `Who inside TEMAŞ owns daily demand forecasting and software purchasing.`

## 10. Bottom line

The contractor-led route is now sufficiently plausible to deserve PMR alongside the university-led route. It should **not** replace the current beachhead until direct interviews show that the contractor owns enough of the decision, economics and deployment process to make the segment genuinely more homogeneous and faster to win.
