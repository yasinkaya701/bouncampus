# Beachhead Channel & Reference-Network Evidence

**Research date:** 2026-10-05
**Status:** Secondary/public research. This assesses Disciplined Entrepreneurship's same-sales-process / word-of-mouth condition; it does not prove referrals or sales conversion.

## 1. Why this matters

Disciplined Entrepreneurship beachhead logic expects a segment in which customers:
- use essentially the same product;
- can be reached through a similar sales process;
- participate in a meaningful reference/word-of-mouth network.

The current research shows stronger public evidence for a **catering-operator industry network** than for a dedicated cross-university food-service-manager network.

## 2. Contractor / catering-industry network — visible national structure

### YESİDEF
Yemek Sanayicileri Dernekleri Federasyonu (YESİDEF) is described publicly as the umbrella organization bringing together catering-industry associations across Türkiye.

Source:
https://www.buysad.org.tr/yesidef-hakkinda/

The public description states that the federation works on:
- food safety;
- hygiene;
- quality standards;
- sustainability;
- common sector problems;
- cooperation with public institutions, private sector and civil society.

### Public-sector engagement

Et ve Süt Kurumu official news, 24 June 2025:
https://www.esk.gov.tr/tr/17006/Turkiye-Yemek-Sanayicileri-Dernekleri-Federasyonu-Genel-Mudurumuzu-Ziyaret-Etti

The YESİDEF board met the General Manager of the public institution to discuss sector issues.

### Implication

The contractor/catering side has a visible infrastructure for:
- peer exchange;
- industry issue coordination;
- public-sector engagement;
- potential reference propagation across member associations/operators.

This does **not** prove that BOUNCAMPUS can sell through YESİDEF or that members share software references. It makes that channel testable through PMR.

## 3. Broader professional food ecosystem

Other visible professional networks include:
- regional catering-industry associations under YESİDEF;
- food engineers / technical professional networks;
- chef federations such as TAFED;
- broader food-industry federations such as TGDF.

These reinforce that institutional-food-service professionals are not isolated actors, but they are not automatically a direct B2B software channel.

## 4. University-side network evidence is weaker

Public research clearly shows recurring university organizational structures:
- SKS departments;
- Food Services / Dining Operations branches;
- food engineers / dietitians;
- procurement/control committees.

However the research has **not yet identified equally strong public evidence of a dedicated national peer/referral network specifically for university food-service decision owners**.

Possible networks/contextual channels may include:
- university SKS benchmarking;
- YÖK / sustainability initiatives;
- GreenMetric communities;
- university administrative workshops;
- contractor overlap across institutions.

But the DE word-of-mouth condition should remain `TESTING`, not assumed.

## 5. Why contractor-led beachhead may score better on DE channel criteria

A contractor-led segment could have:

```text
shared industry associations
+ standardized professional language
+ multi-site operator portfolios
+ repeated public procurement participation
+ internal cross-site references
```

Potential consequence:
- one successful site could create an internal reference inside a contractor portfolio;
- contractor peers may share practices through industry associations;
- sales messaging could be more homogeneous if economics/workflow are standardized.

Again, this only matters if PMR confirms the same product and buyer structure.

## 6. University-led beachhead still has advantages

University-side selling may offer:
- direct access to campus calendar/context/data;
- strong mission alignment with sustainability;
- institutional pilot/research relationships;
- easier local access for the team at Boğaziçi;
- ability to validate user needs close to the problem.

Therefore channel/network evidence alone should not select the beachhead.

## 7. PMR questions to test reference behavior

Ask every serious interview:
1. Who else in another institution/company deals with this same planning problem?
2. Who do you call when you want to compare food-service practice with peers?
3. Which professional association, working group or supplier network do you trust?
4. Have you adopted a process/software because another university/site recommended it?
5. Which institutions or catering companies do you view as operational benchmarks?
6. Would you introduce us to one peer who sees this problem differently?
7. If a pilot worked here, whose reference would make a second site take it seriously?
8. Does HQ standardize tools across sites or do project managers choose independently?

## 8. Evidence interpretation

### Strong beachhead signal
Interviewees independently name overlapping peer networks / references and provide introductions.

### Weak signal
Each institution/site operates independently and buying decisions start from zero with no meaningful peer influence.

### Contractor-specific strong signal
A contractor HQ can mandate/recommend one tool across many sites and local operators trust an internal reference site.

## 9. GTM experiment hypothesis

A future non-product experiment could be:

```text
one validated pilot site
→ one documented operator reference
→ referral to second same-archetype site
→ measure whether access/sales process materially shortens
```

Do not call this repeatable GTM until it happens.

## 10. Bottom line

Current secondary evidence favors the **contractor ecosystem** on visible industry-network structure, while the university-side cross-institution reference network remains less proven.

This slightly strengthens the contractor-led beachhead hypothesis, but the decisive questions remain:
- does contractor HQ own/influence the decision?
- is the workflow standardized?
- can one product serve multiple sites?
- does contractor capture enough value to buy?

Those require PMR.
