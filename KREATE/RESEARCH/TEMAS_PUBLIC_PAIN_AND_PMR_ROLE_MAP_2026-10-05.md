## 3. Explicit public waste/planning signal

A current public TEMAŞ social post uses a hypothetical example of a small excess quantity repeated across **55 kitchens** to illustrate cumulative resource loss. The post explicitly links waste prevention to:
- determining need correctly;
- controlling delivery quantities;
- supplier coordination;
- planning production according to demand.

### Why this matters

This is stronger than generic `we care about sustainability` messaging because it points to an operational mechanism:

```text
need estimation
→ purchase / receiving quantity
→ production planning
→ repeated site-level excess
→ aggregate waste/resource loss
```

### Boundary

This does **not** prove:
- TEMAŞ currently suffers from a specific forecast error level;
- TEMAŞ lacks adequate planning software;
- a particular TEMAŞ site needs BOUNCAMPUS;
- TEMAŞ would buy a forecasting product.

It only makes the problem class highly relevant for direct PMR.

## 5. PMR role map — ask for roles, not guessed individuals

### T-01 — HQ Operations / Production Planning
Primary questions:
- Is daily quantity planning standardized across sites?
- Who owns the forecasting/planning method?
- Which data are centralized at HQ?
- Which systems are mandatory across projects?
- Is there an internal planning KPI for overproduction/shortage?

### T-02 — HQ ERP / IT / Digital Transformation owner
Primary questions:
- What catering ERP/planning system is used?
- Does the system forecast quantities or only execute staff-entered plans?
- What APIs/exports exist?
- Who can approve a new software pilot/integration?
- Is site-level university data contractually shareable with HQ?

### T-03 — HQ Finance / Cost Control
Primary questions:
- Which contracts expose TEMAŞ to excess-production cost?
- Which contracts pay per served/turnstile meal vs notified/produced quantity?
- How is site gross margin affected by demand error?
- Are emergency-production and waste costs visible in management accounting?

### T-04 — Quality / Food Safety
Primary questions:
- What surplus can safely be reused/reallocated?
- Which waste is mandatory discard?
- What controls must a production recommendation never bypass?
- Which measurement method is accepted internally?

### T-05 — Boğaziçi local project / central-kitchen operation
Highest-value questions:
- Who chooses tomorrow's quantity?
- What time does it freeze?
- What is the batch structure?
- What signals are used?
- What happens if North Campus or another campus demand differs from plan?
- What does university instruct vs contractor decide?
- What is recorded after service?

### T-06 — second university/institution project
Purpose:
- determine whether Boğaziçi workflow is TEMAŞ-standard or client-specific;
- test same-product/same-sales-process condition.

## 6. Best introduction path

The highest-integrity route is:

```text
Boğaziçi Food Services PMR
→ ask who at TEMAŞ owns daily production planning
→ request introduction to local TEMAŞ project/production lead
→ ask local lead who owns the method/system at HQ
→ then HQ PMR
```

This is better than guessing employees from social profiles.

A generic corporate collaboration contact is publicly available at the company's official contact page, but PMR should first pursue role-owned referrals where possible.

## 7. Contractor-led beachhead test

TEMAŞ becomes a stronger candidate beachhead only if PMR shows all of:

1. a repeated quantity decision across multiple sites;
2. enough standardization that one product can serve those sites;
3. contractor captures meaningful operational/economic value from better planning;
4. a centralized or repeatable software procurement route exists;
5. client data/integration permissions are manageable;
6. incumbent ERP leaves a material decision gap;
7. one pilot can credibly lead to a second deployment.

If these fail, remain university-site-led.
