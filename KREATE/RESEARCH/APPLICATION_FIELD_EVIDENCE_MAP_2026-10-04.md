# A. Define the Problem

## Currently defensible public context

- Boğaziçi reports **48,251 kg total food waste for 2025** (`E-PUB-001/002`).
- It is a multi-campus dining operation with six dining halls and published high meal volume (`E-PUB-003`).
- Meals are centrally produced in North Campus kitchen and distributed into the wider dining operation (`E-PUB-004`).
- The current two-year service procurement contains **3.13M meal units** across six campuses (`E-PUB-005`).
- Comparable Turkish universities show that meal quantity can be a real pre-service planning decision with shortage/excess consequences (`E-PUB-006/007/008/012/013/016`).

## Still hypothesis at Boğaziçi

- demand mismatch is a material cause of the 48,251 kg total;
- excessive production is a material fraction of measured waste;
- daily production quantity is adjustable;
- the current decision process lacks sufficient information;
- shortage risk causes a safety buffer.

## Counter-evidence / alternative causes that must remain visible

Boğaziçi employee satisfaction data and academic literature make other causes plausible:

- quality/taste;
- portion adequacy;
- menu preference;
- plate waste;
- preparation/process loss;
- storage/service issues.

`E-PUB-021` is a service-quality signal, not a waste-causality study.

## Best problem logic before PMR

Safe structure:

```text
high-volume recurring food service
+ production/allocation must be planned before final demand is fully observed
+ wrong quantity can create surplus or shortage
+ local materiality of this mechanism is being tested
```

Do not write:

> Boğaziçi wastes 48 tonnes because it cannot forecast demand.

That causal statement is unsupported.

## Evidence needed to close field strongly

At least several concrete incidents establishing:

```text
expected demand
planned/produced quantity
actual served demand
what remained / what ran out
root cause
what decision could have changed outcome
```

# B. Define Your Beachhead Market

## Secondary evidence supports narrowing by workflow

Turkish university dining is not one homogeneous operating model.

Observed public archetypes include:

- contractor forecasts daily demand and bears excess exposure;
- university determines quantity using reservations + expected walk-ins;
- reservation-first campus/meal allocation;
- turnstile/payment-based realized demand;
- no/limited reference information supplied to contractor;
- integrated cafeteria-card infrastructure.

Therefore `universities` or `large universities` alone is not a strong DE beachhead.

## Current best candidate segment

> Medium-to-large Turkish universities with centrally coordinated/institution-governed dining, repeated high-volume meal services, a reachable production/allocation decision, measurable actual consumption, and a residual demand-planning gap not already solved by reservation/incumbent systems.

This remains a **market-segmentation hypothesis**.

## Disciplined Entrepreneurship checks still missing

### Same product
Need PMR to show the same decision product solves the same job across institutions.

### Same sales process
Unknown because quantity owner/economic buyer can be university, contractor or joint.

### Community / word of mouth
`E-PUB-020` supports direct SKS peer benchmarking, but a dense Food Services buyer community is not yet validated.

## Strong external repeatability targets

- GTÜ — same demand/quantity/shortage mechanism publicly visible;
- İTÜ — realized-demand settlement and replenishment logic;
- Bilkent — foundation-university cafeteria-management structure;
- Amasya — reservation-first architecture useful as a contrasting segment.

## Beachhead invalidation conditions

Narrow or change segment if:

- buying process differs fundamentally institution to institution;
- reservation systems already solve the main pain;
- quantity decisions are contractor-owned but the proposed buyer is university;
- no repeated community/reference path exists;
- the product requires bespoke workflow for every campus.

# C. Persona

## Public evidence identifies role families, not the actual persona

Current role pattern across universities:

```text
Food Services / cafeteria operations management
+

+
contractor/service operations
```

Boğaziçi has a real public governance anchor:

- Aygül Demir Yolasığmazoğlu — Yemek Hizmetleri Şube Müdürü (`E-PUB-011`).

But her public title does **not** establish that she personally sets production quantities.

## Current primary persona hypothesis

> The person accountable for translating uncertain expected demand into a meal-production/allocation plan while protecting service continuity.

Possible real role:

- Food Services manager;
- food engineer;
- contractor project manager;
- kitchen/production manager;
- joint decision workflow.

## Prioritized purchasing criteria — hypotheses only

Candidate priorities to rank through PMR:

1. service continuity / no unacceptable shortage;
2. decision arrives before freeze point;
3. low staff burden;
4. reliability and missing-data behavior;
5. human control / explainability;
6. fit with contractor/current systems;
7. measurable operational/economic effect;
8. privacy/procurement burden;
9. sustainability/reporting value.

Do not state this order as fact until the real persona makes tradeoffs.

## Persona fields that cannot be invented

- age;
- personal motivation;
- personal frustrations;
- buying authority;
- budget ownership;
- willingness to adopt;
- exact success KPI.

The form asks for a realistic persona, so unvalidated items should be explicitly marked as working assumptions.

# D. PMR Hypotheses

The three strongest form hypotheses remain:

## H-A — controllable decision

> In large, centrally coordinated university dining operations, an identifiable operational role determines or approves meal-production quantities before actual demand is known, and that quantity remains adjustable until a defined operational freeze point.

## H-B — material decision-linked mismatch

> Demand uncertainty and the current production-planning process repeatedly create meaningful mismatches between food prepared and food actually needed, producing avoidable edible surplus and/or shortages that can be affected by changing the production decision.

## H-C — asymmetric risk / safety buffer

> Food-service operators intentionally maintain a production safety buffer because shortages create a more immediate operational risk than surplus, and this buffer is determined mainly through historical experience and fragmented information rather than a consistently integrated decision process.

Each is decomposed into smaller killable assumptions in:

`COMPONENT_ASSUMPTIONS_AND_KILL_GATES_2026-10-04.md`.

# E. PMR — Interview 1/2/3

## Official form minimum

The form requires **at least 3 customer interviews**.

Repo target of 12–16 is an internal evidence-quality target, not an organizer requirement.

## Highest-information first three

### Interview 1
**Food Services governance/workflow owner**

Goal:
- identify real quantity owner;
- freeze point;
- contractor split;
- data/workflow;
- pilot route.

### Interview 2
**Current contractor local project/production owner**

Goal:
- actual forecasting heuristic;
- ingredient/batch commitment;
- excess/shortage economics;
- current ERP/tooling;
- local vs central planning.

Current named local 2026 contact is not reliably public; obtain referral through Food Services rather than using a historical name.

### Interview 3
**Food engineer / daily production-adjacent operator**

Goal:
- last concrete mismatch;
- safety buffer;
- exact records;
- root-cause split;
- current workaround.

## Best fourth/sixth interviews

- BUCard/IT data owner;
- waste measurement owner;
- same quantity-decision persona at GTÜ/İTÜ/Bilkent or another second institution.

## Key Insights standard

Do not write:
> Interviewee liked the idea.

Write:

```text
last incident
+ current behavior
+ decision owner/timing
+ consequence
+ surprise
+ which hypothesis changed
```

No `E-INT-*` evidence may be created before the real conversation occurs.

# F. Define Your Proposed Solution

## What secondary evidence permits

Technical/market evidence supports a plausible solution category:

> human-reviewed, uncertainty-aware decision support around a named institutional dining decision, using existing data first and adding measurement only where a decision-critical gap exists.

## What must remain conditional on PMR

The exact first action may be:

- total production quantity;
- campus allocation;
- second-batch release;
- reservation no-show/walk-in correction;
- portion adjustment;
- menu/quality action.

Do not hard-code the application solution to production forecasting if PMR falsifies H-B.

## Existing alternatives that must be acknowledged

- experienced staff and spreadsheets;
- reservation systems;
- cafeteria/card/POS/ERP systems;
- catering production/ERP software;
- Winnow;
- Leanpath;
- campus sustainability/reporting platforms such as Emissary.

## Claims that are no longer defensible as differentiation

- first AI food-waste system;
- competitors only measure waste;
- nobody forecasts production;
- first university sustainability platform;
- digitizing catering is unique.

## Remaining differentiation hypothesis

```text
university-specific operational context
+ decision timing / freeze-point fit
+ uncertainty / WITHHOLD
+ human approval
+ source provenance
+ post-decision outcome verification
```

This is a **product thesis**, not yet customer-validated differentiation.

# G. Strong Value Proposition

Organizer material explicitly values strong value proposition.

Current evidence is insufficient to choose one final QVP because the persona's top priority and economic beneficiary are unknown.

Possible PMR-tested QVPs:

- fewer edible surplus portions/kg without more shortages;
- lower marginal production/ingredient cost;
- fewer emergency substitutions/early sellouts;
- less manual planning/reconciliation effort;
- stronger university-contractor auditability;
- measured sustainability evidence.

Do not lead with climate score if the operator's top priority is continuity, or with cost if the operator does not capture the savings.

# H. Climate impact

Before a real pilot:

Safe:
- food waste is globally climate/resource relevant;
- prevention is generally preferable to creating avoidable waste and managing it later;
- the system is designed to measure physical reduction before converting to climate impact.

Unsafe:
- `48,251 kg x generic factor = BOUNCAMPUS savings`;
- achieved CO2/water savings;
- a universal kg-food-to-CO2 factor.

Preferred evidence chain:

```text
measured edible food prevented
-> food category/recipe
-> documented LCA factor/range
-> DERIVED_ESTIMATE
```

See `CLIMATE_IMPACT_CONVERSION_HIERARCHY_2026-10-04.md`.
