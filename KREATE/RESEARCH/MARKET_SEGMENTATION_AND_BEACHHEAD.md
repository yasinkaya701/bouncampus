# Türkiye University Market Segmentation & Beachhead Criteria

**Research date:** 2026-10-01  
**Status:** Secondary research + segmentation hypothesis. Not TAM/SAM/SOM and not willingness-to-pay evidence.

## Executive conclusion

Do **not** define the beachhead as "all Turkish universities" or even simply "public universities." The relevant segmentation variable is the **decision workflow and measurement maturity** of institutional dining operations.

A stronger candidate beachhead is:

> **Medium/large universities with centralized or coordinated dining production, measurable waste/service-volume data, an operator who can change production quantities before service, and enough digital/administrative maturity to support a pilot.**

Boğaziçi is a useful test case because public sources show multi-campus dining, a central production context, measured food waste, digital meal/menu infrastructure and broader sustainability governance. Whether the production decision is actually reachable remains PMR.

# M-01 — National institution count is context, not market size

`PUBLIC SOURCE`

YÖK's 2024 University Monitoring and Evaluation General Report states that Türkiye had **208 actively teaching higher-education institutions**. YÖK also referenced 208 universities in 2025 reporting.

Sources:

- https://www.yok.gov.tr/Documents/2024/universite-izleme-ve-degerlendirme-genel-raporu-2024.pdf
- https://www.yok.gov.tr/Sayfalar/Haberler/2025/depremden-etkilenen-universiteler.aspx

**Boundary:** the existence of 208 institutions does not imply 208 potential buyers, suitable pilots, independent software budgets, or identical dining workflows.

Do not use `208 × assumed annual price` as TAM without a justified addressability methodology.

# Segmentation axis 1 — Dining decision structure

## S1 — Centralized/coordinated high-volume dining

Characteristics:

- central kitchen or coordinated production;
- repeated daily meal services;
- measurable production/serving quantities;
- enough volume that forecast errors can matter materially.

**Highest-priority segment for current hypothesis.**

Why: one decision improvement can affect many meals and the feedback loop is frequent.

## S2 — Contractor-operated but institution-governed dining

Characteristics:

- contractor performs production/service;
- university SKS/procurement/food-services unit defines service/quality/contract rules;
- quantity risk and settlement mechanics may sit with either side.

Priority depends on contract semantics.

Critical PMR question:

> Who economically and operationally benefits if overproduction falls, and who bears shortage risk?

## S3 — Fragmented multi-vendor retail/canteen ecosystem

Characteristics:

- many independent outlets;
- no central production decision;
- heterogeneous POS/inventory systems.

Lower initial priority because integration and decision ownership are fragmented.

## S4 — Reservation/subsidy-driven institutional dining

Characteristics:

- meals may be booked/subsidized;
- show/no-show behavior can create uncertainty;
- reservation records may be a strong predictor if accessible.

Potentially attractive, but must not be assumed for Boğaziçi.

# Segmentation axis 2 — Measurement maturity

## L0 — No reliable service-level measurement

- waste not weighed consistently;
- produced/served quantities unavailable;
- reporting mostly qualitative.

Likely needs a measurement pilot before predictive decision support.

## L1 — Basic counts / spreadsheets

- some produced/served/waste data exists;
- manual reconciliation;
- weak provenance.

Potentially attractive if workflow pain is high and integration is light.

## L2 — Digital transactions + periodic waste measurement

- transaction/card/POS-like counts;
- menu/calendar records;
- waste tracking;
- no integrated decision model.

**Strong candidate maturity level for first predictive pilot.**

## L3 — Specialist food-waste or forecasting system already in place

- mature waste measurement;
- forecasting/root-cause tools;
- intervention workflow.

Only attractive if BOUNCAMPUS demonstrates a meaningful gap such as institutional context, contract coordination, cross-domain verification or lower integration burden.

# Segmentation axis 3 — Sustainability/digital-governance maturity

## G0 — Compliance/reporting minimal

Value proposition likely starts with measurement/governance; this overlaps directly with established sustainability platforms and is not BOUNCAMPUS's strongest current differentiation.

## G1 — Active sustainability office / GreenMetric participation

Has recurring evidence/reporting process but may still rely on manual coordination.

Potential demand for provenance/workflow exists, but Türkiye already has direct competitors in this category.

## G2 — Mature monitoring / ISO / BMS / ranking performance

Examples in Türkiye publicly demonstrate BMS/BEMS, ISO 50001 and advanced sustainability reporting.

Here, a basic dashboard is least valuable. Candidate opportunity is:

- cross-system decision prioritization;
- operational recommendation;
- intervention verification;
- domain-specific optimization.

Boğaziçi appears publicly closer to this mature end in water/energy governance, though internal tool maturity must be verified.

# Candidate ideal customer profile for dining pilot

Score a candidate campus on these dimensions:

| Criterion | Weight concept | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated meal volume | High | actual service volume |
| Central decision point | Critical | named owner + workflow |
| Quantity discretion | Critical | operator/contract confirmation |
| Waste materiality | High | measured stream, preferably stage-separated |
| Data availability | Critical | historical service-level fields |
| Shortage measurability | High | early sellout/substitution/service metric |
| Pilot permission path | High | named approver/data owner |
| Current-tool gap | Critical | concrete unsupported decision |
| Integration burden | Negative | IT/security/procurement requirements |
| Economic incentive alignment | Critical | who saves / who pays / who risks shortage |

Do not assign numeric scores until interviews define the evidence.

# Proposed PMR sampling strategy

A useful first external sample is **variation-seeking**, not statistically representative.

After Boğaziçi workflow interviews, select 2–3 Turkish institutions that differ on purpose:

### Case A — Large public / centralized dining

Goal: test whether the same quantity-decision problem repeats under public procurement constraints.

### Case B — Foundation university / more agile procurement

Goal: test buyer speed, tool stack and willingness to pilot where procurement may differ.

### Case C — sustainability/digital leader

Goal: test whether mature systems already solve the problem and whether decision/verification still creates incremental value.

This sample is for **mechanism comparison**, not national market sizing.

# Beachhead promotion gate

The team should not call institutional dining a validated beachhead until evidence supports all of the following:

1. **Repeated pain:** multiple operators describe recent demand/production mismatch incidents or another repeated decision failure.
2. **Actionable control point:** a named role can change the decision before the outcome.
3. **Measurable outcome:** produced/served/waste + shortage guardrail can be captured.
4. **Current-tool gap:** existing software/spreadsheets do not already solve the decision adequately.
5. **Incentive alignment:** reducing mismatch creates value for a party that can sponsor/adopt the solution.
6. **Pilot feasibility:** data/security/procurement friction is acceptable for a bounded test.
7. **Repeatability:** at least one or two additional institutions show a similar enough workflow to support productization.

If any critical gate fails, narrow or change the beachhead before adding more product scope.

---

# Buyer / user / beneficiary separation

Do not collapse these roles.

Potential roles to test:

- **daily user:** production planner / food engineer / kitchen manager;
- **decision owner:** SKS/Food Services manager or contractor operations lead;
- **data owner:** university IT/BİD, card/payment system owner, sustainability/Zero Waste owner, contractor system owner;
- **economic buyer:** institution/administrative unit or contractor depending procurement model;
- **beneficiary:** university budget, contractor margin, sustainability office, students, environment.

A product can have strong student/environmental benefit and still fail commercially if the economic buyer does not capture enough value.

# Market sizing rules for later agents

Before building TAM/SAM/SOM, collect at least:

```text
number_of_addressable_institutions_by_segment
meal_volume_distribution
outsourced_vs_inhouse_share
centralized_vs_fragmented_service_share
existing_specialist_system_penetration
procurement_budget_owner
credible annual price / contract structure
sales-cycle constraints
integration/pilot costs
```

Without these, any monetary market size is speculative.

# Immediate implication for Boğaziçi

Boğaziçi is not valuable merely because it is one university among ~208.

It is valuable as a **mechanism test** because public evidence suggests:

- material/reported dining operations;
- measurable waste;
- multiple campuses/service regimes;
- centralized production context;
- public sustainability maturity;
- enough institutional complexity to expose real data-governance and decision-workflow constraints.

The next step remains primary workflow validation, not broader top-down TAM arithmetic.
